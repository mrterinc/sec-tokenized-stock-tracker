SEC Innovation Exemption Comment Tracker: Tokenized NMS Stock

SEC comment-letter tracker · File No. 4-927 · Release No. 34-106402

On 17 September 2026 the Securities and Exchange Commission issued an order, which it calls the Innovation Exemption, exempting certain venues that trade blockchain versions of US listed stocks through automated liquidity pools from the definition of an exchange, and certain firms that supply those pools from the definition of a dealer. The order took effect on issue and runs until 17 September 2031, subject to conditions. It also asks for public comment on how the exemptions should be changed. The comment file has no closing date. Read the order (PDF) or browse the comment file on sec.gov.

This tracker records every comment letter on that file and, for each part of the order a letter engages, two things: its stance on the order as granted, and the direction in which it would move the exemption. The second is recorded because letters that ask for changes can ask for opposite things, some for wider relief and some for stricter conditions, and a single support-or-oppose scale cannot separate them.

Background

What the order does  the venues, the liquidity providers and the conditions

In the United States a system that brings together the orders of many buyers and sellers of securities is an exchange, and must register as one or operate as a regulated alternative trading system. A firm in the business of buying and selling securities for its own account is a dealer, and must register as a broker-dealer. Section 36 of the Securities Exchange Act lets the Commission exempt persons or transactions from any provision of the Act, by order and on conditions, where it finds that to be in the public interest and consistent with the protection of investors. An exemptive order of this kind takes effect without first being proposed for comment. The order on this comment file is one.

It concerns Tokenized NMS Stock: a token on a blockchain that represents an ordinary exchange-listed US stock (NMS stands for national market system), made either by the company that issued the stock or by an unaffiliated third party. Tokens that only track a stock's price, such as linked notes or derivatives, are excluded. The venues it covers, Tokenized Securities Venues (TSVs), trade such tokens through automated market makers: smart contracts that hold a pool of two assets and price each trade from the ratio between them, with no order book. Access to a TSV is permissioned, so only approved wallet addresses can trade, but its smart contracts must be public and run on a public, permissionless blockchain.

A TSV that meets the order's conditions is exempt from the definition of an exchange. The conditions include being a U.S. person; posting a public notice describing the venue, its fees, risks and procedures; giving the issuer of a third-party-tokenized stock 30 days' notice, with the issuer able to object; verifying that each token carries the same rights as the underlying share; limits on how many stocks it may trade and on its share of each stock's trading volume; publishing trade data within ten minutes; stopping trading when the stock's listing exchange does; no lending or margin; and keeping records available to Commission staff. A separate exemption from the definition of a dealer covers Covered Firms that supply the pools from their own capital, on conditions of their own. Both exemptions run to 17 September 2031.

The order follows the January 2026 staff Statement on Tokenized Securities in distinguishing issuer-sponsored from third-party tokens. Its final section asks ten numbered questions about modifying, extending or ending the exemptions. On the day the order was issued, Chairman Atkins (statement), Commissioner Peirce (statement) and Commissioner Uyeda (statement) issued statements on it. A companion tracker covers the Commission's separate proposal, Regulation Crypto Assets, which concerns offerings of crypto assets rather than trading of tokenized stock.

Methodology

How the coding works  the two axes, the three raters, and what keeps the record stable

Every letter is read and, for each part of the order it engages, two values are recorded. Stance is Support, Oppose or Conditional relative to the order as granted: Support accepts that part as it stands, Conditional accepts it subject to changes, and Oppose rejects it as granted. Direction is Loosen, Tighten, Clarify or As-granted. Loosen makes the exemption available to more venues, securities, participants, volume or time, or eases a condition; Tighten narrows it or adds a condition or required content; Clarify asks the Commission to say how an existing condition applies without changing what it requires. A request framed as a clarification that would, if granted, take a class of conduct or person outside the order is recorded as loosening, and a request that the notice or the published data include something new is recorded as tightening however the letter describes it.

Each letter is read three times, by three Claude raters given different instructions: one reading in good faith and in context, one taking only what the letter says explicitly, and one setting the framing aside to ask what the request would do if granted. All three votes are kept and the label is the majority, so the level of agreement can be measured rather than asserted; it is reported further down this page. Every stance carries a verbatim quote from the letter and every direction call carries its reason, and both are published, letter by letter, on the evidence page.

The coding rules were set before any letter was coded, tested on a pilot batch and then fixed. Every row records the version it was coded under; if the rules change, every letter already coded is checked against the new rule in the same pass. Source documents are archived when they arrive, with a checksum, and attachments are archived with the letter they belong to. Letters are numbered by date of filing, and numbering is stable: a late-posted letter takes the next free number. A form letter is one row, with its number of copies recorded beside it.

Each letter also carries a Frame, which records where the changes it asks for would be made: in this order (on-instrument), in another rulemaking (other rulemaking), in the regulation of trading markets or systemic risk (market structure or systemic risk), or in no particular rule (non-specific). Frame does not change how any position is coded.

Stance and direction are separate questions, and this bar answers the second: how a letter would change the exemption, not whether it is friendly to the order. Asking that other kinds of securities be admitted is a loosening ask; asking that the venue publish more data is a tightening one. A letter that asks for both is Mixed-direction; one that asks only for the Commission to say how an existing condition applies is Clarifying; one that supports what it engages as granted is As-granted; and one that engages no part of the order is shown as n-a rather than left off the bar. Posture is computed from the nineteen parts of the order (E1 to E19); the whole-letter reading (GEN) and the two code lists (C1, C2) are not parts of the order and are left out. E1I.A
TSV definition and scope

A Tokenized Securities Venue (TSV) is an organisation, association or group of persons that provides AMM Liquidity Pools for permissioned participants and sets the standards for who may trade. Being a TSV exempts it from the definition of “exchange”, so it registers neither as an exchange nor as an alternative trading system. In an automated market maker (AMM) a smart contract holds a pool of two assets and prices trades from the ratio between them; there is no order book. Letters here concern what the definition reaches, including who “provides” a pool and how the order bears on pools that no one operates.

How requests framed as clarifications are coded. A request that the Commission confirm something, where the confirmation would take a class of conduct or person outside the order, is recorded as loosening. A request that resolves an ambiguity without changing what is caught is recorded as clarifying.

GEN carries the most split calls, 4. Two kinds of split recur. On definitions such as “permissionless”, a letter that supports the principle but calls the wording wrong reads as Conditional to two raters and as Oppose to the one who takes the text literally. Both are recorded as they fall rather than smoothed over.
